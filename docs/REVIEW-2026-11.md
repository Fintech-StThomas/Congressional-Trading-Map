journalist / campaign-finance review that used the live site for real reporting tasks and spot-checked 17 figures against

## 2. Plan

Each item: rationale · acceptance · owner type · dependencies · risk · real data.

### P1. Street address published in liability text (privacy, critical)

- **Rationale.** `/m/P000197` shows "Equity Line of Credit on 2640 Broadway," (FD 10075701, Schedule D) in the page,
  the liabilities CSV and the holdings JSON, in the current release and in every archived release. `rules.redact`
  applies the house-number patterns (A2, A3) only to text that names real property; A1 needs a street suffix and
  "Broadway" has none. This breaks the privacy decision.
- **Acceptance.** Liability and asset text with a house number after "on", "at" or "secured by", or with any
  digit-led address shape the A2 rule recognises, is redacted to `[street address redacted]`, while account numbers,
  years, share counts and "529" stay. A new holdings-validator check fails the build on a digit-led address token in
  any published liability or asset text. Unit tests with the Pelosi row and negatives. The real-data run reports how
  many rows the new rule redacts, and a reviewer reads a sample of 20 redacted and 20 untouched digit-bearing rows.
- **Owner type.** pipeline. **Depends on** nothing. **Risk:** over-redaction of legitimate asset names (checked by the sample).
- **Real data:** yes.
- **Owner decision (not an agent's):** archived releases already hold the address. `docs/RELEASE-OPERATIONS.md`
  ("Withdrawal policy") describes withdrawing a release; whether to withdraw the affected past releases is yours.

### P2. Amended House trade reports counted as new trades (correctness, critical)

- **Rationale.** The House reprints amended transactions in a later PTR marked "Filing Status: Amended"; the source
  dataset gives the reprint a new event id, so `pipeline/trades.py:119-125` keeps both, with `amended:false`. For
  Pelosi this adds 5 duplicate trades, turns trades public in 19–30 days into 50, 108 and 186 days
  ("public over 45 days": 5 shown, 1 genuine), and overstates the 2020 Amazon tie by $500,002–$1,000,000. McCaul
  shows 106 same-key pairs across filings. The `/delays` totals carry an unknown share of this.
- **Acceptance.** (a) A measurement first: how many current House events share an exact key (member, transaction
  date, owner, action, amount band, ticker or asset text) with an event in a *different* filing, and whether the
  dataset carries a row-level filing status or a transaction id that marks the reprint. (b) Fold only on an exact,
  filed signal (a row marked Amended, or the same House transaction id); never on similarity. The folded event keeps
  the earliest public date for `lag_days`, `amended:true`, and cites both filings. (c) Same-key pairs without such a
  signal are kept, counted in `meta`, and not silently merged. (d) A validator check that no member has two current
  trades with the same exact key in different filings unless one is folded or explicitly counted. (e) `/method` and
  `/delays` wording match what is done. Unit tests with fixtures shaped like the Pelosi rows.
- **Owner type.** pipeline, then docs and web wording. **Depends on** the measurement. **Risk:** high (changes trade
  counts, sums and timing site-wide). **Real data:** yes.
- **Owner decision:** if the dataset carries no row-level status, folding needs our own reading of PTR text. That
  method choice comes back to you with the measured counts before it ships.

### P3. Missing shown as zero on member pages (correctness, high)

- **Rationale.** (a) "Residence-secured: $0–$0" when no liability could be identified as residence-secured
  (`liabilities_residence_secured.n = 0` partly because the type text is cut at a PDF line break). (b) The net-worth
  chart draws a bar from $0 when the low bound is null (`HoldingsSection.tsx:26,52-53`). (c) "Activity by year" says
  "2026: 0 money records, data complete" while the ledger has 33 ties with 2026 records; the chart counts only
  records on full ties. (d) The 2026 cycle is not marked partial.
- **Acceptance.** (a) says "not identified in the filing" when n is 0, and the note beside the net-worth range says
  residence mortgages are included unless flagged; (b) an unbounded-below marker instead of a $0 bar; (c) counts
  every record (full ties and ledger) per year, or is relabelled to say what it counts; the open cycle says
  "in progress". Pure helpers with unit tests; smoke test on the sample site; 390 px and print checked.
- **Owner type.** web. **Depends on** nothing. **Risk:** low. **Real data:** no (checked against the live bucket).

### P4. Money totals that leave out the largest ties and mix support with opposition (sourcing, high)

- **Rationale.** "Outside spending 61 ties · $277,469" under "Further ties" omits the three largest opposing ties
  ($2.48 M) and adds $39,689 of support to $237,780 of opposition. "PAC contributions $12,762,426" likewise leaves
  out the 14 largest ties. A reporter would quote these as totals.
- **Acceptance.** Each money kind shows one all-in total (full ties plus ledger) with its record count; outside
  spending is split into *for* and *against*; any "smaller ties only" subtotal is labelled as such. Totals equal the
  sums in `wall.json` (a test asserts it). Pure helper with unit tests.
- **Owner type.** web. **Depends on** nothing. **Risk:** low. **Real data:** no.

### P5. Wording and citation precision (sourcing, medium)

- **Rationale (review B, section C and items 8, 13, 16, 17).** `/connect` says "Nancy Pelosi reported trading stock
  in Apple" (the spouse's trades) and "Nancy Pelosi gave money to …" (a committee gave). RSS titles omit the owner.
  The embed card's "Stock trades $27.9 M–$113.3 M" reads as a position (it is buys plus sells). `/method` and
  `/status` still say "late"; `/method` says amended trades are marked (not true for item P2) and lists tie kinds not
  in the build (employee and lobbyist donations). Outside spending says "exact dollars" but drops cents. The evidence
  drawer says "The 34 other records are in the same source filings" (they are in other filings, years apart).
  Citations lead with the Hugging Face dataset rather than the official filer.
- **Acceptance.** All wording lives in `lib/copy*.ts`; household and committee are named where the actor is not the
  member; trade totals read "bought and sold combined" with buys and sells shown apart; "late" is gone except as a
  JSON field name (documented); tie kinds not in the build are marked so; the drawer sentence is accurate; citations
  name the Clerk DocID or FEC committee id first and the extraction dataset second. Unit tests on the copy helpers.
- **Owner type.** web. **Depends on** P2 for the amended sentence. **Risk:** low. **Real data:** no.

### P6. Release, cache and feed hardening (ops, medium)

- **Rationale (review A, items 2–5, 8, 9).** (a) Withdrawn releases can keep rendering from Vercel's Data Cache
  (pinned reads use `force-cache`); the runbook omits purging it. (b) Any made-up 64-hex release id costs an uncached
  Worker call and R2 reads on a free 100,000-requests-a-day Worker. (c) `/feed/w.xml` accepts ids in any order, so
  one watchlist has factorially many cache keys. (d) Error responses under `/releases/<id>/` are sent as immutable for
  a day. (e) `LDA_API_KEY`, `FEC_API_KEY` and `SEC_USER_AGENT` reach every branch dispatch. (f) Release docs and
  `docs/data-dictionary.md` still describe the archive as not yet live.
- **Acceptance.** (a) runbook step plus a tombstone check with a short revalidate before a permalink is served;
  (b) the site accepts a release id only if `releases/index.json` lists it, and the Worker negative-caches a missing
  descriptor; (c) a non-canonical watch query answers 308 to the canonical one; (d) only 200 responses carry the
  immutable policy; (e) the three keys come from the `production` environment or are passed only on `main`, and the
  daily job still publishes without them; (f) docs updated. Tests for (b)–(d).
- **Owner type.** ops and web. **Depends on** nothing. **Risk:** medium for (e): a misconfigured environment stops
  FEC purpose groups on `main`; the pipeline already degrades without the key. **Real data:** no (branch run checks (e)).

### P7. Campaign receipts per cycle (journalist-critical gap, high)

- **Rationale.** The first question about a member, "how much did they raise this cycle, and from whom", has no
  answer. The FEC candidate summary the loans already come from carries total receipts, individual (itemised and
  unitemised) and other-committee contributions, transfers, disbursements, cash on hand and coverage end date per
  cycle, keyed by the exact candidate id.
- **Acceptance.** `docs/data-dictionary.md` is changed first; member files gain per-cycle receipts fields from the
  same source (blank is null, never zero), with the coverage end date; the "Campaign money" section and full file show
  a per-cycle table with CSV and a link to the FEC candidate page; values match OpenFEC
  `/candidate/{id}/totals/` for three members in the real-data run. Validator checks for non-negative, null-safe fields.
- **Owner type.** pipeline then web. **Depends on** nothing. **Risk:** medium (new contract fields). **Real data:** yes.

### P8. Leadership PAC money in, totals and "through" date (journalist-critical gap, medium)

- **Rationale.** Leadership PAC panels show spending only, with no receipts, no total disbursements, no
  contributions-to-candidates total and no coverage date, and their two spending figures disagree without a
  reconciling line (PAC to the Future 2026: operating $5.70 M vs purpose groups $7.73 M vs FEC operating $5.83 M
  through 14 Oct 2026).
- **Acceptance.** Per PAC-cycle receipts, disbursements, contributions to other committees and coverage end date from
  OpenFEC `/committee/{id}/totals/` (batched, cached, failure-tolerant: the daily job publishes without them); a line
  saying how the figures relate; contract documented first. **Owner type.** pipeline then web. **Depends on** P7's
  data-dictionary edit (same section, run sequentially). **Risk:** medium. **Real data:** yes.

### P9. Earmarked pass-through counted as PAC money (correctness, high)

- **Rationale.** Some PACs forward individuals' earmarked money as non-memo 24K rows with no "earmark" text (Senate
  Conservatives Fund C00448696, House Freedom Fund C00552851, NORPAC C00247403, RJC PAC C00345132), so
  `pac_money` counts it as the PAC's own: Cruz 2018 overstated by about $113,000 (one $36,039 row, over the $5,000
  per-election limit); House Freedom Fund to Jordan about $24,500. Roughly 100–180 giver-recipient pairs a cycle,
  $2.3–4.2 M, under 1% of PAC dollars but 3–7x on those ties. Separately a gift the giver coded 24G is absent from
  pas2 (AFSCME to Pelosi 2005, $5,000).
- **Decision (lead).** Exclude the 24K rows of a reviewed list of conduit PACs (exact ids) from `pac_money`, counted
  as their own exclusion and said on the site; their genuine direct gifts are dropped too, with that stated (matching
  them against recipient line 11C via OpenFEC is a follow-up). A validator report of any kept row over $5,000 from a
  non-party, non-candidate giver, with a reviewed allow-list. Document the 24G gap. Correct the Cruz 2018, Cruz 2022
  and RJC/SCF paragraphs in `pipeline/README.md`; make the weball cross-check compare like with like.
- **Owner type.** pipeline, docs, small web wording. **Risk:** medium (tie totals fall for affected members).
  **Real data:** yes (`main` run, since branch runs build without the FEC key).
